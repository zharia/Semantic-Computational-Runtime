**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Baseline:** `034a9c0` (v0.0.3)

**Status:** **PRODUCTION READY** (single-instance; see Known Gaps)

---

## 1. Objective

The v0.0.4 increment's stated goal was to make HyrxMQ *boringly reliable*:
continue to work correctly under malformed input, concurrency, resource
exhaustion, connection failure, process termination, filesystem failure,
authentication/authorization failure, sustained and burst load, slow consumers,
large messages, many connections, persistence, TLS, restart and Kubernetes
lifecycle management — and to remove any architectural feature gaps without
expanding surface area.

Clustering remains an explicit non-goal; Kubernetes is the orchestration
substrate.

## 3. Performance result

Fair comparison — all three brokers in Docker on one bridge network, each
published to `127.0.0.1` (identical docker-proxy path), each `--cpus 4
--memory 2g`, identical AMQP configuration, one compiled Go load generator, 7
rotated repetitions, medians.

Geometric mean of relative throughput (`fastest/rate`; 1.000 = fastest in every
cell):

| | HyrxMQ | RabbitMQ 4.x | LavinMQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **overall (80 cells)** | **1.041** | 1.244 | 6.918 |
| concurrency 1 | **1.007** | 1.324 | 7.675 |
| concurrency 4–8 | **1.059** | 1.223 | 8.749 |
| concurrency 16–32 | **1.039** | 1.225 | 5.194 |

- HyrxMQ is **19.5% ahead of RabbitMQ** and **~5.6× ahead of LavinMQ** on the
  overall geomean.
- HyrxMQ wins **every concurrency band** and **3 of 4 workloads**
  (publish, pubget, confirm). LavinMQ wins fanout.
- Cell tally: **fastest 50 / tied 5 / behind 25**; narrowest loss 1.03×,
  widest 1.31×.
- Peak observed: publish 64 B at 32 connections **676,490 msg/s**; publish
  1 KiB **592,326 msg/s**; `basic.get` 16 KiB **24,710 msg/s**.

Progression of HyrxMQ's overall geomean across the increment:
**2.30 → 1.064 → 1.041** (initial → after protocol/TCP/stall fixes → after
three optimization rounds).

**Caveat.** This is a single-host (AMD Ryzen 5 3600, 12 logical), single-client,
three-image result. It establishes relative behaviour here and does **not**
support a "fastest in the world" claim.

Full detail: `benchmarks/perf/three_broker/REPORT_FINAL2.md`.

## 4. Defects found and fixed in this increment

The benchmarks, interop suite and Kubernetes lifecycle test surfaced real
defects (each fixed and regression-covered):

| # | Defect | Impact | Fix |
|---|--------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Serial one-connection serving | A 2nd client handshake timed out; only 1/16 concurrent pairs connected | Enabled event-driven multiplexing (`event_driven_serving()` = true) |
| 2 | Protocol-header echo before `connection.start` | amqplib / Java / Go desynchronized (hang, 501) | Removed the echo (amqp0-9-1.xml §1.4.2.2) |
| 3 | No async push delivery | `basic.consume`-first then publish delivered 0 messages | Added `drain_pushes()` reusing the one delivery emitter |
| 4 | Auto-delete connection-close race | Racy 404 on a just-declared queue at ≥4 connections | Scoped auto-delete to the closing connection's consumer queue |
| 5 | Dose-loop frame stall | Intermittent ~15 s pubget stall | Drain buffered frames past the read budget |
| 6 | Auto-ack delivery-tag leak | 10-min soak RSS +18.9% | `_chan_alloc_tag_auto` (no per-tag binding); RSS now +0.06% |
| 7 | Missing `TCP_NODELAY` | Nagle added delayed-ACK latency to every round trip | Set on accept (verified via `strace`) |
| 8 | PID-1 SIGTERM ignored | Container SIGKILLed after full grace period (exit 137) | Exit-on-signal shim; K8s `preStop` `kill -TERM 1` |
| 9 | Redundant payload copies | Large-payload publish and per-frame overhead | Zero-copy method read, move semantics, direct-recv-into-codec, exact reply reserve |

## 8. Sign-off

HyrxMQ v0.0.4 meets the increment's objective for single-instance production
deployment: it is correct under adversarial input and concurrency, bounded
under resource pressure, durable across process kill, observable, secure by
default, deployable on Kubernetes with correct lifecycle behaviour, wire-compatible
with four independent AMQP clients, and **the fastest of the three brokers
tested** under a fair, reproducible benchmark.

Remaining gaps are documented and non-blocking. Downstream work (the next
rounds) should begin from this baseline.

**Verdict: SHIP (v0.0.4).**
