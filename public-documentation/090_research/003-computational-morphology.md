# Research: Computational Morphology

SCR treats morphology as a potential general computational bridge between patterns, structure, geometry, topology, representation, and observation.

## Core relationship

```text
Pattern
   ↕
Morphology
   ↕
Geometry
   ↕
Topology
   ↕
Representation
```

The relationship is bidirectional.

Patterns can produce morphology.

Morphology can be analysed to recover patterns.

## Why this is broader than graphics

A morphology can describe:

- biological form;
- spatial organization;
- graph structure;
- agent structure;
- physical configuration;
- evolving topology;
- semantic organization;
- renderable form.

A mesh is only one possible representation.

## Morphology and dynamics

A dynamic system may change its morphology:

```text
Morphology(t)
      ↓
Dynamics
      ↓
Morphology(t + Δt)
```

This allows structural change to become a computational variable.

## Morphology and rendering

Rendering may consume a morphological interpretation:

```text
Semantic Structure
      ↓
Morphology
      ↓
Render Representation
      ↓
Observation
```

But observation can also feed pattern extraction:

```text
Observation
      ↓
Pattern Extraction
      ↓
Morphological Interpretation
```

This closes the loop.

## Research questions

Important questions include:

- Can morphology have an independent semantic representation?
- How should topology be represented?
- Can pattern extraction and morphology synthesis share infrastructure?
- How should structural equivalence be defined?
- How can morphology be compiled?
- How should dynamic morphology be optimized?
- How can symbolic and spatial representations interoperate?

## Status

Computational morphology is a research and architectural direction. It should not be interpreted as a claim that a complete general morphology compiler already exists.
