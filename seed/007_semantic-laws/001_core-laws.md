# Core Semantic Laws

These laws are the initial Seed law vocabulary. A law is not considered formally proven merely because it appears here.

## Semantic Field Primacy

Higher-level computational structures and physical manifestations MUST derive from or explicitly map to Semantic Field structure.

## Identity Persistence

A valid transformation or representation change MUST preserve semantic identity unless identity change is explicitly part of its contract.

## Representation Independence

A semantic meaning MUST NOT depend on a particular concrete representation.

## Contract Preservation

A valid transformation MUST preserve the semantic contract applicable to the transformed object.

## Constraint Preservation

A transformation MUST preserve constraints that its contract declares invariant.

## Context Preservation

A transformation MUST preserve relevant context or explicitly declare the context change.

## Provider Non-Authority

A provider realizes a semantic contract; it does not redefine that contract.

## Lower-Layer Non-Authority

Representation, implementation and execution mechanisms do not silently acquire authority over semantic meaning.

## Abstraction Closure

A valid composition or refinement should itself be representable as a semantic object.

## Semantic Equivalence

Substitution is valid only when the relevant semantic equivalence contract is satisfied.

## Provenance Preservation

Where provenance is required by contract, transformations preserve sufficient lineage to reconstruct the relevant semantic ancestry.
